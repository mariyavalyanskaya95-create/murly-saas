3Reading: 8 laws that sound fake readingteacher + student

Read the text. Were your guesses right? Then do True or False and answer the questions.

8 laws that sound fake (but aren't)

1. Singapore: no gum. Since 1992, you can't sell or import chewing gum in Singapore. The government wanted clean streets and working train doors. There is one loophole: some medical gum is allowed.

2. Venice: don't feed the pigeons. In St Mark's Square, feeding pigeons is banned, and tourists can get a fine. The birds damage the old buildings, so the city is strict about it.

3. Switzerland: never just one guinea pig. Guinea pigs are social animals, so Swiss law says you can't keep only one. If you have a guinea pig, it needs a friend.

4. Greece: leave your heels at home. Since 2009, high heels are not allowed at some ancient sites, like the Acropolis. Thin heels can damage the old stones.

5. Denmark: name approved? Danish parents usually choose a baby name from an official list. If they want something more creative, they need special permission.

6. Italy: no sandcastles? A few years ago, a small Italian beach town reportedly banned building sandcastles, because big holes and towers got in the way of other people on the beach.

7. Japan: the waistline law. Under the so-called "metabo law", many adults aged 40 to 74 have their waist measured at yearly health checks.

8. Czechia: be visible! Every car must carry a reflective vest, and in the centre of Prague drinking alcohol is banned in many streets and squares. Tourists get busted all the time.

6Rules: must, have to, allowed to grammarteacher + student

Read the rules. Then do A and B. Last: tell me 3 rules at your work or in your building in Prague.

must / mustn't

a strong rule

You must carry a reflective vest. You mustn't feed the pigeons.

have to / don't have to

it's necessary / not necessary

Danish parents have to choose from a list. You don't have to buy a vest for a bike. (= not necessary, it's OK)

can / can't, be allowed to

permission

You can't sell gum. You aren't allowed to wear heels there. Are we allowed to drink here?

Past

had to / was allowed to / couldn't

I had to pay a fine. We weren't allowed to take photos. He couldn't park there.

Careful! mustn't = it's forbidden. don't have to = it's not necessary. Totally different!

A. Choose

3It's free entry, so you ___ buy a ticket.

5We ___ to take photos inside the museum, so I have none.

2Yesterday I ___ (have to) pay for a new tram ticket.

Відповіді A: 1 mustn't · 2 have to · 3 don't have to · 4 had to · 5 weren't allowed · 6 Are. B: can't · had to · aren't allowed to · don't have to. Типова помилка українців: mustn't ≠ don't have to (не треба). Минулого часу в must немає: had to.
