6Big grammar revision: our 5 tenses grammarteacher + student

Present Simple

V / V+s

Habits, facts. I eat pasta before matches.

Present Continuous

am/is/are + -ing

Now, these days. I'm cutting down on sugar.

Past Simple

V2

Finished, when? I cooked dinner yesterday.

Past Continuous

was/were + -ing

Background. I was chopping onions when the phone rang.

Present Perfect

have/has + V3

Experience, result now. I've never baked bread. We've run out of milk.

Which tense? Quick check

1I ___ breakfast every morning at 7.
2Shh, Dad ___ dinner, don't disturb him.
3We ___ pizza last night.
4I ___ onions when I cut my finger.
5I ___ sushi twice in my life.
6Look! The pasta ___ over!
7When I got home, my brother ___ the last slice of pizza.
8Water ___ at 100 degrees.
9Oh no! We ___ out of eggs.
10While I ___ the table, Mum was stirring the soup.

Простая шпаргалка на русском:
1. Present Simple: обычно, всегда, факт («ем пасту перед матчем»). Маркеры: always, usually, every day.
2. Present Continuous: прямо сейчас или в этот период («сейчас готовит», «на этой неделе ограничиваю сахар»). Маркеры: now, look!, these days.
3. Past Simple: закончилось, есть «когда» (вчера, в субботу, 2 дня назад). Маркеры: yesterday, ago, last.
4. Past Continuous: процесс-фон в прошлом («резал лук, когда позвонили»). Маркеры: while, at 8 yesterday.
5. Present Perfect: опыт или результат сейчас, без точного времени («никогда не пёк хлеб», «закончилось молоко»). Маркеры: ever, never, already, just, yet.
Главная ловушка: Past Simple против Present Perfect: если есть «когда» (yesterday) только Past Simple.
Приём: нарисуйте линию времени: прошлое | сейчас, и ставьте каждое время на линию вместе с учеником.
Ответы: have · is cooking · ordered · was chopping · have tried · is boiling · was eating · boils · have run · was laying.

The worst birthday dinner ever

My name is Danny, and I'm a goalkeeper. I'm good at stopping balls, but I'm terrible at stopping disasters in the kitchen. My mum says I have burned more pans than anyone she knows. She's probably right.

Last March it was my girlfriend Chloe's twentieth birthday, and I decided to cook her a special dinner. I don't usually cook anything more complicated than toast, but I watched a ten-minute video online and thought, “How hard can it be?”

The menu was ambitious: mushroom soup, salmon with lemon butter and a chocolate cake from scratch. I went to the supermarket at eight in the morning. While I was choosing the salmon, a little boy recognised me and asked for a selfie. I said yes, of course. But while we were taking photos, somebody walked away with my trolley.

By four o'clock I was finally cooking. The soup was simmering, the salmon was roasting in the oven and I was whisking cream for the cake. Everything was going perfectly. I even felt like a real chef. Then my phone rang. It was the coach, and we talked about Saturday's match for twenty-five minutes.

When I came back, the kitchen was full of smoke and the smoke alarm was screaming. The salmon looked like a piece of charcoal. The soup was stuck to the bottom of the pan. And my dog was sitting quietly in the corner and licking his lips. The cake was gone.

Chloe arrived at eight. She looked at the kitchen, then at me, then at the dog, and started laughing. “You've never cooked for me before,” she said, “and now I know why.” We ordered a large pizza, sat on the floor and ate it straight out of the box. She still says it was the best birthday dinner she has ever had.

Since then I have taken five cooking classes, and I have learned to make a decent risotto. Right now I'm practising for this year's birthday. Chloe is a bit nervous, and the dog is very excited. Oh, and I have bought a fire extinguisher. Just in case.

My brother was reading the recipe out loud and I was chopping the vegetables. Suddenly he shouted, “The fish needs to marinate for six hours!" And our parents were coming in two hours. We panicked and put the fish in the microwave. Five minutes later the whole flat smelled like a fish market. Our neighbour rang the doorbell and asked if we were OK. When our parents came in, we were opening all the windows. Dad smelled the air and asked, "Have you cooked fish?" Mum tried a piece and smiled very politely. Then she said, "This is the most unusual dinner I have ever eaten." In the end we ordered sushi and everyone loved it. Now we go to cooking classes every Wednesday. This week we are learning to cook fish. My brother says he still dreams about that fish. And Dad has already booked a table at a restaurant for their next anniversary.

The midfielder who cooks

Jake is nineteen and plays as a midfielder for a youth team in Manchester. Everybody knows him as a fast runner, but in the dressing room he has another nickname: Chef.

He started cooking two years ago, when he moved into a flat with two teammates. At first, they were living on frozen pizza and toast. After a month, Jake was feeling tired all the time, and the coach noticed it.

"You run on what you eat," the coach told him. Jake didn't know how to cook anything more difficult than an omelette, so he watched videos, called his grandmother and burned a lot of pans.

Now he cooks every evening. His signature dish is chicken pasta with garlic, spinach and a splash of cream. He is also experimenting with weird combos these days: his latest invention is a banana and peanut butter pancake with a pinch of salt.

"Cooking is like football," Jake says. "You need a plan, good timing and a team. And if you make a mistake, you don't give up. You try again on Monday."
